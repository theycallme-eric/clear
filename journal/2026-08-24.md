Progressive overload research came back as a real spec and got promoted into four requirements.

The interesting part: **the spec changed M0.** It flagged that the old schema stored weight as
a bare number with no unit — lb vs kg — and called that blocking. It is. So the baseline schema
now carries a unit, and M1's execution screens capture section-level effort and AMRAP partial

That's the argument for stubs, made concrete. A stub is not an unfinished requirement, it's an
honest one. If OVR's details had been invented in v0.1, DATA-01 would have shipped without a
unit column and M1 would have spent months not recording data M3 needs.

## Review pass one — and an architectural correction

Six requirements marked. Three of them (DS-01, DS-02, DS-06) plus GEN-05 turned out to share one
cause: the design system has moved since these were written. Several themes now, not two, and the
orange/blue swap is no longer the relationship it was. Gated rather than guessed.

**The most valuable note was a question, not a correction.** On GEN-03: *"what happens when there's
a failure or things don't load/render?"* The document had error handling everywhere in pieces —
error taxonomy in CORE-01, ErrorState in DS-05, typed failures in GEN-03 — and nowhere as a
contract. Nothing said *every view defines loading, empty, error, populated*. That is exactly the
kind of gap that ships as an app which feels broken without any single requirement being wrong.
Now CORE-04, and a standing criterion on all UI work.

OVR-04 got "I don't really know what this one does," which was a writing failure. Rewritten to open
with what a deload is and why the app would ever suggest one, before any mechanics.

## Requirements approved · IA built

All 51 reviewed. The six sent with notes were the only ones with issues — the rest were approved
in the tool, which I couldn't see because the copy step failed. Approval is a checkpoint, not a
freeze; the IA work, the schema pass, and the incoming generation review will all move things.

**IA closes the composition gap.** Requirements said what screens do, the design system said what
components exist, and nothing said which components compose which screen. Fourteen screen contracts
now, each with route, guard, entry and exit, the four CORE-04 states, its component tree, and the
requirements that own it.

Two things worth keeping from building it:

The **three paths into Review** are genuinely different — fresh generation, regeneration (discards
the previous workout), and favorite restart (replays a snapshot, no generation call at all). REV-01
has to handle all three and its acceptance criteria currently only describe the first.

The **component vocabulary rule** may be the most useful thing in the document: anything a screen
needs that isn't in the vocabulary is a new DS requirement with a ticket, never an inline one-off.
That's the mechanism that stops twenty UI tickets from producing twenty private button variants.

Five open questions at the end — mostly about interactions that don't exist yet and may not need to.

## IA questions answered

All five, and two changed requirements rather than just documentation.

**"Workout is a focus mode"** is the one with teeth. No in-app navigation out of an active session
— and the reasoning Eric gave is better than the one I'd have written: every route reachable
mid-workout is a chance to strand a session in an ambiguous state, so removing the exits removes
the whole class of bug. Focus is the felt benefit; state safety is the engineering one.

The distinction worth preserving: **the trap is on navigation, not on the user.** Closing the tab or
backgrounding the phone still persists and resumes. Trapping the person would be hostile; trapping
the router is just correct.

**Repeated favorites** stay separate sessions with a comparison card threading them. Landed on
FAV-02, which already owned personal bests and completion history.

## One thing his phrasing surfaced

He said "regenerated" where I'd asked about "restarted," and the slip is interesting. Regenerating
from Review discards a workout silently — no trace. But *"regenerated three times before starting"*
is the app being told, precisely, that generation failed to produce something worth doing. It's the
only honest signal of generation quality obtainable without asking the user a question.

Nothing records it. Flagged for the schema pass: capture it, or decide deliberately not to. Cheap
now, invisible later.

## D6 — swapped exercises are never persisted

Traced while writing the v0.4 change set, and it is worse than a design gap.

Generation saves the original prescription. `useExerciseSwap` mutates React state only — no write.
`handleStartWorkout` records a timestamp and navigates — no write. The only post-save writes to
`exercises` are `weight_logged` and `exercise_notes` at completion.

So: swap Deadlift for Romanian Deadlift, log 3×8 at 185, and the database records **3×8 at 185 on
Deadlift**. Set logs attach by `exercise_row_id` to rows describing exercises the user never did.

Silent corruption of the exact table progressive overload reads. OVR-01's load anchors would compute
deadlift capacity from RDL sets and never know they were wrong.

This moves the prescribed/revised/performed model from architectural preference to **correctness
requirement**, and it gives SES-01 a regression test with a specific reproduction rather than a
principle.

## Convergence with the outside review

Three rounds, and the disagreements resolved cleanly rather than being split:

- **Limitations** — they proposed structured injury fields, I pushed back that it contradicted their
  own "no clinical ontology" boundary, they revised to user-authored constraints. Better than either
  starting position. The rule that fell out: *no constraint is ever inferred from free text.*
- **Metadata** — they proposed eleven attributes, I flagged authoring cost as the unasked question,
  they cut to five and dropped `fatigue_sensitivity` with good reasoning (fatigue is a property of
  a prescription, not an exercise). The coverage-gating idea is theirs and it is the thing that
  makes the whole safety layer shippable incrementally.
- **Intensity** — stays 1–10 user-facing, resolves internally into six capped dimensions. The
  compounding bug gets fixed without pushing backend complexity onto someone walking into a gym.

The one genuinely blocking question is not technical: **who reviews the metadata.** If there is no
qualified fitness reviewer, the honest move is to ship factual metadata plus explicit user
exclusions and not claim a safety layer. That is a positioning decision.
