Colors — Roles
The system has two primary roles, and they are roles rather than colours: structure is orange in CLEAR, purple in Vapour, chartreuse in Signal and grey in Mono. Naming them by hue is the fastest way to write code that only works in one skin.
Structure
Cards, strokes, surfaces, borders, accent bars, labels. Everything that frames content.
Interaction
CTAs, tappable icons, links, headings. Everything the user acts on, or the system announces.

A skin sets these two hues and five more values; every ramp and alpha step derives from them. Which is why the two roles are the whole contract — get the pairing right and the rest of the palette follows.
The pairing is not symmetric. Structure appears at full strength or at 10%, never in between; interaction lives at 40–60% over near-black, where a hue can lose its identity. That is why a hue can work as structure and fail as interaction — Signal's chartreuse goes olive at 40% — and why the Alpha Behaviour card tests interaction hues but exempts structure.
